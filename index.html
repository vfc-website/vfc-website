Investment Thesis

East Africa's missing middle.

Too large for the bank's SME desk. Too small for a global fund's cheque. That is where East Africa's best businesses are waiting.

Picture a dairy processor outside Eldoret. Twenty years ago it was one cooler and a pickup. Today it buys from three thousand farmers, employs two hundred people, and turns a healthy profit. Its founder is approaching sixty — and every important decision still runs through him.

Across Kenya, Tanzania, Uganda and Rwanda there are thousands of businesses like this one. They have outgrown the founder's notebook, but the institutions that could help them grow rarely come knocking. Banks lend against land, not potential. Large funds need larger cheques.

We exist for that business. We acquire a controlling or majority stake, step into management, and install the systems, people and discipline that let it scale beyond its founder — then exit to a buyer who will carry it further.

01

Operator-led ownership

We take active management roles after acquisition and build leadership depth that outlasts our involvement.

02

Deal-by-deal SPVs

Each acquisition is its own vehicle. Investors see the asset, underwrite it, and decide — no blind pool.

03

Value from operations

Returns come from margin recovery, formal systems and better distribution — not from financial engineering.

04

Exit by design

Trade sale, secondary buyout or listing — we plan the route to the next owner from the first day.

Why nowA young, urbanising population is buying more packaged and processed food every year — most of it still made by founder-run firms.

Logistics & Cold Chain

Temperature-controlled storage, freight and last-mile networks — the infrastructure behind food security and medicine supply across the region.
